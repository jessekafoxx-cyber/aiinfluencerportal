### What
A short, practical walkthrough of setting up the social media accounts your AI avatar will actually post from. Gregory covers which platforms to prioritise, the dedicated Gmail address trick, what to put in the avatar's profile, how to attach your bridge page as the link in bio, and how to post to Facebook, Instagram and TikTok in a single click using Buffer.

### Why
- Your avatar needs a real home on the platforms before any content strategy matters — this is the plumbing.
- Instagram and TikTok are where the reach is right now, and Facebook comes free with the same upload, so you get three times the reach for the same effort.
- A dedicated Gmail keeps the avatar completely separate from your personal accounts, which makes handover clean if you ever sell the asset later.
- The link in bio is the only bridge between the content and the offer. Without it, every view is wasted.
- Follower count is not the goal. The algorithm no longer serves your content mainly to followers, so chasing followers is the wrong metric — impressions and profile visits are what matter.

### How
1. **Create the accounts.** Go to Instagram, then TikTok, then Facebook. Use your avatar's name as the handle and upload the avatar's photo. Start posting immediately — do not wait for followers.
2. **Set up a dedicated Gmail address** for the avatar first, and register all three accounts to it. This keeps everything separate and streamlined, and makes the asset transferable.
3. **Add the profile information.** Upload a normal, believable profile photo of your avatar — home office wall, in the car, on a podcast — and write the bio (use the bio generator shown in the course).
4. **Add your bridge page as the link in bio.** In GoHighLevel go to *Funnels → your funnel → Edit bridge page*, copy the page URL, and paste it as the single link in your avatar's bio.
5. **Keep it to one link.** One avatar per offer, one link in bio. Do not stack multiple links.
6. **Post everywhere in one click.** Use Buffer to schedule to Facebook, Instagram and TikTok at once — roughly $5–6/month for one account, around $18/month for all three. You can load a full month of content in a single sitting.

### Notes
- There are places to buy aged accounts, and Gregory does use them for advanced work — ignore that entirely when starting out. Create fresh accounts and start.
- Do not over-think the setup. There is no magic formula here; the content and the engagement campaign do the heavy lifting.
- The profile picture and setting should look ordinary and human. Overly polished "brand" imagery hurts believability.
- Buffer is the recommended tool, but any multi-platform scheduler that supports all three networks works the same way.

### What
A full over-the-shoulder build of a Meta engagement ad campaign — the paid push behind your organic posts. Gregory explains why almost every "organic" viral account is really running engagement ads, shows live proof of reaching nearly 100,000 people for under $300, then walks through the entire campaign setup: budget, ad set structure, audience, placements, using existing posts, and turning off every enhancement Meta tries to sneak on.

### Why
- Meta does not make money from people using Instagram for free. It makes money from advertisers — so paid content gets pushed, and organic content mostly does not.
- Engagement campaigns run at roughly **$3 per 1,000 people reached**. Even $5/day puts you in front of ~1,500 new people daily, and performance improves the longer it runs.
- It gives your posts the initial traction they need to keep climbing instead of dying in the first hour. This is exactly what Hormozi, Gary Vee and every large account do behind their best posts.
- It builds the warm engaged audience that your later direct sales campaigns retarget — so this campaign is the foundation, not an optional extra.
- It compresses your timeline. Organic-only progress is slow; a few dollars a day makes progress happen in weeks instead of months.

### How
1. **Set up Ads Manager.** Connect your avatar's Facebook page and Instagram account as connected assets in Business Manager (page name or URL, then follow the prompts).
2. **Create the campaign.** New campaign → name it "Engagement" → choose manual setup → Continue.
3. **Set the daily budget.** $5, $10, $25, $100 — whatever you can sustain. More spend equals more reach; roughly $3 per 1,000 people.
4. **Build the ad set structure.** Gregory plans ~90 days of content and groups posts by week — "Week 1", "Week 2", etc. Each day, add that day's 3 posts into the current week's ad set, then duplicate the ad set for the next week.
5. **Engagement type:** select **Post Engagement**. **Performance goal:** *Maximise engagement with post*.
6. **Audience.** Top five English-speaking countries (US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand) unless you are targeting a local or non-English market. Set minimum age 22–25 for high-ticket buyers. Leave everything else blank and let Meta work.
7. **Placements: manual.** Turn ON only Facebook Feed, Instagram Feed, Facebook Reels, Instagram Reels. Turn off everything else, including Stories. The interface jumps around — double-check before saving.
8. **Create the ad.** Name it (Post 1, Post 2…) → **Use Existing Post** → pick the Instagram or Facebook post → Continue.
9. **Clean the ad up.** Untick **Multi-Advertiser Ads**, remove the call-to-action button, and click Edit to **turn off every enhancement**. The goal is to keep people on Meta commenting on your post, not clicking away.
10. **Duplicate and scale.** Duplicate with original settings for each additional post, swap in the new post, re-check enhancements are off, then Publish. Add each day's new posts as you create them.

### Notes
- Watch enhancements closely — Meta re-enables them on duplicated ads. Check every single one before publishing.
- The caption matters more than the button. "Comment [keyword] below" is what drives the Meta automation and profile visits.
- If your ad account gets restricted, cheap Fiverr "Meta ad ban" fixes work, or simply open a new account. Agency ad accounts exist for advanced use — not needed at this stage.
- The lower your CPM, the better your content is. Improving content lowers cost and increases engagement at the same time.
- Direct sales campaigns come later and require a very different, direct style of creative. They retarget everyone this engagement campaign warms up — so run this one first.
- Gregory has screenshots of every step in the campaign build, so learners can follow along frame by frame.
